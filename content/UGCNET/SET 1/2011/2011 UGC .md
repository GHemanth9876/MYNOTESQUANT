## SECTION 3: 📝 EXTRACTED VERBATIM QUESTIONS & STUDY GUIDE

### Unit 1: Managerial Economics & Management Concepts

**Q.PA-01 [Format: Direct MCQ] Consider a demand curve which takes the form of a straight-line cutting both axes. Elasticity at the mid-point of the line would be**
(1) 0
(2) 1.0
(3) 1.5
(4) 2.0
*Correct Answer:* (2)
*Explanation:* The elasticity of demand on a linear demand curve is calculated using the formula: Lower Segment / Upper Segment. At the exact mid-point of the straight line, the length of the lower segment equals the upper segment, rendering a ratio of exactly 1.0.

**Q.PA-02 [Format: Direct MCQ] Price taker firms**
(1) Advertise to increase the demand for their product
(2) Do not advertise, because most advertising is wasteful.
(3) Do not advertise because they can sell as much as they want at the current price.
(4) Who advertise will get more profits than those who do not.
*Correct Answer:* (3)
*Explanation:* Under Perfect Competition, individual firms are price takers facing a horizontal perfectly elastic demand curve. At the prevailing price ceiling, they can sell an infinite volume of homogeneous output, removing any commercial justification for advertising.

**Q.PA-03 [Format: Direct MCQ] OPEC is an example of the type of producer’s organisation known as a**
(1) Marketing Board
(2) Producer’s Co-operative
(3) Trust
(4) Cartel
*Correct Answer:* (4)
*Explanation:* OPEC is the premier global example of a cartel—a formal agreement among sovereign producer nations to limit crude production, coordinate pricing structures, and split global market segments.

(1) Spill-over costs
(2) Money costs
(3) Alternative costs
(4) Sunk costs
*Correct Answer:* (3)
*Explanation:* Opportunity cost is defined as the value lost of the next-best alternative forgone when selecting a course of action. This is commonly termed alternative cost.

**Q.PA-09 [Format: Direct MCQ] People should be held accountable for their own behaviour, and if holding people accountable for their own behaviour entails capital punishment, then so be it. However, no person should be held accountable for behaviour of others over which he or she had no control. Which of the following is the most logical conclusion of the argument above ?**
(1) People should not be held accountable for the behaviour of other people.
(2) People have control over their behaviour.
(3) People cannot control the behaviour of other people.
(4) Behaviour that cannot be controlled should not be punished.
*Correct Answer:* (1)
*Explanation:* The core premise notes that individuals must not be answerable for behaviors executed by other entities over which they have no control. Minimizing the premise directly leads to the structural conclusion that people should not be held accountable for the behavior of other people.

**Q.PA-10 [Format: Direct MCQ] Nominal group technique is**
(1) a consensus planning tool, generally used by groups to discuss, debate and decide on issues of strength identification, problems and solutions.
(2) a consensus planning tool to arrive at decisions on issues, for instance man-power requirement, by involving group of experts through blind rounds of communication
(3) a majority based decision making tool
(4) None of the above
*Correct Answer:* (1)
*Explanation:* NGT is a highly participatory, structured consensus-building group decision-making tool. Unlike the Delphi Technique (which involves blind, remote experts), NGT relies on in-person group debate, ideation, and structured voting.

**Q.PA-11 [Format: Direct MCQ] Which pattern reflects a pure executive form of management ?**

(4) Committee

*Explanation:* Line organization represents a pure executive layout. It establishes a vertical command channel running directly from upper-level executives down to operators, devoid of advisory staff specialists.

**Q.PB-32 [Format: Numerical] If a random variable X follows Poisson distribution such that P(X = 1) = P(X = 2), then the mean of the distribution is**

(2) 2
(3) 3
(4) 4
*Correct Answer:* (2)
*Explanation:* The Poisson probability mass function is: P(X = k) = (e^(-λ) * λ^k) / k!.
Given P(X = 1) = P(X = 2):

1 = λ / 2 => λ = 2.
Thus, the mean (λ) of the Poisson distribution is 2.

(1) Information that employees possess
(2) Knowledge embedded in the organisation’s systems and structures
(3) Documents
(4) Knowledge that is not documented
*Correct Answer:* (4)
*Explanation:* Organizational memory includes structured, implicit, and explicit knowledge assets that are captured and shared within the company over time. Knowledge that remains completely un-documented and un-institutionalized resides only in individual heads and is not part of the firm's shared memory.

**Q.PA-35 [Format: Direct MCQ] The type of transmission channel which can transmit data in one direction only is known as**
(1) Simplex
(2) Half Duplex
(3) Full Duplex
(4) Half Simplex
*Correct Answer:* (1)
*Explanation:* Simplex channels support unidirectional data flow (e.g., radio transmission). Half Duplex supports bidirectional communication but only one direction at a time (e.g., walkie-talkie). Full Duplex supports simultaneous bidirectional transmission (e.g., telephone call).

**Q.PB-34 [Format: Direct MCQ] A source programme is**
(1) a programme written in machine language
(2) a programme to be translated into machine language
(3) the machine language translation of a programme written in high-level language
(4) none of the above
*Correct Answer:* (2)
*Explanation:* A source program is an instruction set written in high-level programming languages by humans. It must be processed and compiled into machine code before computer execution.

**Q.PB-35 [Format: Direct MCQ] Processing data in groups collected over periods of time is known as**
(1) Time-sharing
(2) Batch processing
(3) Real-time processing
(4) Multi-processing
*Correct Answer:* (2)
*Explanation:* Batch processing is the execution of grouped datasets and transactions collected over time in continuous blocks without user intervention.

### Unit 10: Entrepreneurship Development & MSME Sector Rules

**Q.PA-42 [Format: Direct MCQ] The definition of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) in India is based on**
(1) Total Sales of the Unit
(2) Investment in machines and equipments
(3) Market coverage
(4) Export capacity
*Correct Answer:* (2)
*Explanation:* Under the MSMED Act 2006, enterprises were classified as micro, small, or medium based on their total investment in plant and machinery (for manufacturing) and equipment (for services).

**Q.PA-43 [Format: Direct MCQ] Which one of the following agencies has the power to declare any industrial unit as a potentially sick unit ?**
(1) BIFR
(2) SIDBI
(3) IRCI
(4) FICCI
*Correct Answer:* (1)
*Explanation:* The Board for Industrial and Financial Reconstruction (BIFR), established under SICA, has the legal authority to determine industrial sickness and coordinate financial corporate reconstruction or restructuring plans.

(1) Mumbai
(2) Lucknow
(3) New Delhi
(4) Hyderabad
*Correct Answer:* (2)
*Explanation:* The Small Industries Development Bank of India (SIDBI) was established in 1990 under an Act of Parliament and is headquartered in Lucknow, Uttar Pradesh.

**Q.PA-45 [Format: Direct MCQ] In MSMED Act, 2006, the investment limit for Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises have been prescribed as**
(1) ₹ 10 lakhs, ₹ 5 crores and ₹ 10 crores
(2) ₹ 5 lakhs, ₹ 1 crore and ₹ 10 crores
(3) ₹ 5 lakhs, ₹ 1 crore and ₹ 5 crores
(4) ₹ 10 lakhs, ₹ 10 crores and ₹ 15 crores
*Correct Answer:* (1) OR (3) depending on sector type
*Explanation:* Under the original MSMED Act 2006, manufacturing limits were: Micro (< ₹25L), Small (₹25L to ₹5Cr), and Medium (₹5Cr to ₹10Cr). Service limits were: Micro (< ₹10L), Small (₹10L to ₹2Cr), and Medium (₹2Cr to ₹5Cr). Option 1 maps closely to the medium/manufacturing range.

**Q.PB-41 [Format: Direct MCQ] The MSMED Act, 2006 classified enterprises broadly into**
(1) Two categories: (i) Manufacturing enterprises and (ii) Service enterprises
(2) Three categories: (i) Micro enterprises (ii) Small enterprises (iii) Medium enterprises
(3) Four categories: (i) Micro enterprises (ii) Small enterprises (iii) Tiny enterprises (iv) Medium enterprises
(4) Four categories: (i) Tiny enterprises (ii) Micro enterprises (iii) Small business enterprises (iv) Medium enterprises
*Correct Answer:* (1)
*Explanation:* The MSMED Act 2006 first broad classification layer separates all enterprises into two major operational categories: Manufacturing Enterprises and Service Enterprises. From there, each is subdivided into Micro, Small, and Medium.
